# Black Amber (bkamber.com) — Business Analysis & Business Plan

## Context
A client, Black Amber, asked for a landing page. Before designing it, we need a senior-BA reading of the business: what it really sells, to whom, how it makes money, and what the website has to prove. Sources: the 6-page company profile PDF, and the brand moodboard image. bkamber.com couldn't be reached from this environment (blocked by the network proxy), so nothing in this plan comes from the live site.

Market figures below are directional and labelled **[assumption]** where they are mine. The client must validate them.

---

## 1. Executive summary
Black Amber is an **Amman-based B2B general-trading and indirect-procurement supplier**. It is a one-stop source of non-core consumables and goods for institutions: office and computer supplies, cleaning and hygiene, hygiene kits, uniforms, furniture, electronics, groceries, medical consumables (not medicines) and printing. It also offers import/export, logistics and consulting on top.

The profile calls it "retail, import, export and consulting". The product mix says otherwise. Hygiene kits, sundries, medical materials "except medicines", and cover-photo cartons marked *National Medical Stores / Alere Medical / "for public use – not for sale"* all point to a **tender-driven institutional supplier**. Its natural core market is Jordan's dense **humanitarian/UN/INGO sector**, plus corporates, healthcare, hospitality, education and government.

**Recommended positioning:** *"Your single, tender-ready supply partner in Jordan: from office desk to field kit, sourced globally and delivered locally."*

**Strategic priorities (12 months):**
1. Focus the 17-item list into 4 service pillars.
2. Win framework agreements with 10–15 anchor institutional accounts.
3. Build "tender readiness" (registrations, compliance documents, QA) as the main differentiator.
4. Use the landing page as a credibility and RFQ (request for quote) engine, not a brochure.
5. Manage working capital tightly. It is the #1 risk in this model.

## 2. What the business actually is (BA diagnosis)
| Profile claim | Evidence | BA interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| "Retail" | No store, no consumer products or pricing | Not B2C retail. It is **B2B supply / wholesale distribution** |
| Import & export | Listed as a service; rebrand moodboard says "Black Amber **Import**" | Import is the sourcing engine behind the supply business. Export is opportunistic |
| Consulting | Generic text, no service definition | Today a credibility add-on, not a revenue line. Reframe as **sourcing & procurement advisory** |
| 17 services | Office, cleaning, hygiene kits, hardware, electronics, furniture, groceries, sundries, uniforms, computer, printing, gifts, medical (non-drug), logistics, import/export, consulting, stationery | Classic **MRO / indirect-spend catalogue**. The value is breadth plus one invoice |
| "Medical materials except medicines" | Explicit exclusion | Deliberate regulatory boundary: medicines need JFDA drug licensing; consumables/devices have lighter requirements |

**Business archetype:** consolidated institutional supplier (one vendor for many low-value categories). It earns a trade margin, wins through tenders and framework agreements, and depends on supplier network, speed, compliance and cash.

## 3. Critical inconsistencies to resolve BEFORE building the landing page
1. **Two brand identities.** The profile uses an orange "B" mark, "BLACK AMBER", and orange on white. The moodboard uses a white "A"-in-square mark, "BLACK AMBER IMPORT", and black with amber accents. → Confirm which identity is final.
2. **Two geographies.** The profile says Amman, Jordan (+962 79 0300 772/773, info@bkamber.com). The moodboard card says Dubai, UAE (+971…, blackamberimport.com, "Mohammed Ali, Managing Director"). That card looks like AI placeholder text. → Confirm HQ and whether a UAE entity or plan exists.
3. **Scope drift.** The "Import" in the new name narrows the brand to importing, while the profile sells supply, logistics and consulting. → Decide whether the brand is an importer or a full supply partner. My recommendation: keep **"Black Amber"** as the master brand and treat import as a capability.
4. **Domain.** bkamber.com vs blackamberimport.com. Pick one and redirect the other.
5. **No proof points.** No founding year, clients, volumes, certifications, team or case studies. For a tender-driven buyer this is the biggest conversion blocker.

## 4. Market analysis (Jordan-centric)
**Macro drivers**
- **Humanitarian hub:** Amman hosts regional and country offices of UNHCR, UNICEF, WFP, IOM, WHO, UNFPA and many INGOs. They buy continuously: hygiene and dignity kits, NFIs, office supplies, IT, furniture, uniforms/visibility items, printing, food parcels.
- **Regional gateway:** Jordan is the land corridor to **Iraq** and, since the 2024–25 political change, to **Syria**, where reconstruction and humanitarian flows are expected to grow. **Aqaba (ASEZA)** offers port and free-zone advantages for re-export.
- **Private demand:** hospitals and clinics, hotels (Amman, Dead Sea, Aqaba, Petra), private schools and universities, banks, telecoms, and corporate HQs. All outsource indirect procurement.
- **Public sector:** Government tenders via the Government Procurement Department and JONEPS e-procurement.

**Headwinds**
- **Donor funding cuts in 2025** (notably US foreign-aid reductions) shrank many humanitarian budgets. That means more price pressure and more competition for fewer tenders.
- Low barriers to entry, so general trading is crowded and margins are thin.
- Long payment terms (30–90+ days) and bid/performance bonds tie up cash.

**Sizing approach** (to complete with client data, not invented here):
- TAM = annual indirect-procurement spend of target institution types in Jordan.
- SAM = categories Black Amber covers × accounts reachable via tenders or direct sales.
- SOM = a realistic win-rate (10–20% of bids **[assumption]**) × average contract value.
- Data sources: UNGM contract-award data, agency procurement plans, JONEPS awards, chamber of commerce lists.

## 6. Value proposition
**For** institutional buyers in Jordan who manage many small, recurring purchase categories,
**Black Amber is** a single tender-ready supply partner
**that** sources, kits, quality-checks and delivers across 15+ categories under one contract and one invoice,
**unlike** fragmented local traders or slow international suppliers,
**because** it combines a global sourcing network (import capability) with local warehousing, kitting and delivery.

**Proof the business must build:** response time to RFQ (e.g. <24h), on-time-in-full %, kit assembly capacity per week, QA/inspection reports, registrations (UNGM, agency rosters), sample client logos and testimonials.

## 7. Restructured offering: 4 pillars (replaces the flat list of 17)
1. **Workplace Supplies:** office supplies, stationery, computer supplies, electronics, office & home furniture, printing services & materials, corporate gifts.
2. **Facility & Hygiene:** cleaning materials, hygiene kits, sundries, uniforms, hardware.
3. **Health & Humanitarian:** medical consumables (non-pharmaceutical), hygiene/dignity kit assembly, groceries & food parcels.
4. **Sourcing, Trade & Logistics:** import/export, global sourcing, logistics & delivery, procurement advisory (the reframed "consulting").

**Signature service to lead with:** **Kitting.** Custom-assembled hygiene, dignity, welcome or onboarding kits. It is higher-margin and differentiated, and it fits the humanitarian and corporate segments.

## 8. Business model & revenue streams
| Stream | Mechanism | Indicative gross margin **[assumption]** |
|---|---|---|
| Product supply (spot RFQs) | Buy-sell margin | 15–30% |
| Framework / LTA contracts | Fixed price list, volume | 8–15% |
| Kitting & assembly | Margin + assembly fee | 20–35% |
| Sourcing on request / import | Commission or margin | 5–10% of order value |
| Logistics & delivery | Per-shipment fee | Cost-plus 10–20% |
| Procurement advisory | Day rate / retainer | High, low volume |

**Cost structure:** cost of goods (largest), freight & customs, warehousing, staff (sales, procurement, warehouse, QA), bid costs and bank guarantees, financing cost of receivables.

**Unit-economics rule:** a contract is only attractive if *gross margin − financing cost of payment days − delivery cost > target contribution*. Build a simple bid calculator for this (see §12).

## 9. Competitive landscape
- **Local general-trading companies** compete on price and relationships. They are weak on documentation and brand.
- **Specialist distributors** (office, IT, medical) are deep in one category but not one-stop.
- **International humanitarian suppliers** are strong on standard kits but slower and pricier for local, fast delivery.
- **E-commerce / B2B marketplaces** handle office basics but no tenders, kitting or compliance.

**Where Black Amber wins:** breadth + local speed + tender compliance + custom kitting. **Where it loses:** pure price on single-category bulk. Avoid those bids.

## 10. Go-to-market plan
1. **Registrations (month 0–2):** UNGM vendor registration, individual agency and INGO vendor rosters, JONEPS, the Amman Chamber of Commerce/Industry, and the relevant JFDA registrations for medical consumables.
2. **Tender pipeline:** daily monitoring of UNGM, agency sites, JONEPS and NGO tender boards. Go/no-go scoring for each tender.
3. **Account-based sales:** target list of the top 50 institutions. Meet procurement officers and offer a "sample kit + price list" pack.
4. **Landing page as conversion engine** (see §14): RFQ form, downloadable capability statement, WhatsApp click-to-chat, bilingual AR/EN.
5. **LinkedIn presence:** project posts (kits delivered, sites served), procurement-focused content.
6. **Referrals:** use framework-agreement wins as references for other agencies.

## 11. Operations model
- **Sourcing:** local wholesalers for fast-moving items. Imports from China, Turkey, UAE, India and the EU for volume and margin. Maintain an approved-supplier list with 2+ sources per category.
- **Warehousing & kitting:** a bonded or regular warehouse in Amman with a kitting line. Optional Aqaba presence for re-export.
- **Quality:** pre-shipment inspection, samples approved before mass order, batch/expiry tracking for medical and hygiene items, certificates of conformity.
- **Fulfilment:** own light fleet plus 3PL for out-of-Amman and cross-border shipments.
- **Systems:** ERP/procurement system for RFQ→PO→delivery→invoice (e.g. Zoho Books/Inventory/Procurement), CRM for the tender pipeline, simple vendor scorecard.

**Core process to document (BA deliverable):** RFQ intake → costing → approval → bid submission → award → PO to supplier → QA → kitting → delivery/POD → invoice → collection.

## 12. Financial framework
The source material has no historicals, so this is a **framework to fill with the client**, not a forecast.
- **Inputs to collect:** last-12-month revenue by category and customer, gross margin by category, average payment days, number of bids and win rate, average contract value, fixed costs.
- **Model drivers:** revenue = (bids × win rate × average contract value) + recurring framework volume + spot RFQs.
- **Working capital:** need ≈ (receivable days − payable days) × daily COGS + inventory + bank-guarantee margins. Plan for a credit facility, invoice discounting or a trade-finance line *before* scaling tenders.
- **Scenarios:** conservative, base and growth, varying win rate, margin and payment days.
- **Targets to set** **[assumption, to validate]:** blended gross margin ≥ 18%, DSO ≤ 60 days, no single client > 30% of revenue, kitting ≥ 20% of revenue by year 2.

## 13. Risks & mitigations
| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Cash-flow squeeze from long payment terms and bonds | High | Trade-finance line, deposit or advance terms on custom orders, reject loss-making payment terms |
| Humanitarian funding cuts | High | Diversify into healthcare, corporate and hospitality, so NGO/UN stays ≤ 50% of revenue |
| Client concentration | High | 30% cap per client, account-based pipeline |
| Thin margins, price wars | Medium | Lead with kitting and service, avoid commodity-only bids |
| Regulatory (medical items, customs) | Medium | JFDA compliance, licensed customs broker, product documentation |
| FX and freight volatility | Medium | Quote validity windows, freight-adjustment clauses, USD-pegged JOD helps |
| Supplier failure or quality defects | Medium | Dual sourcing, pre-shipment inspection, penalty clauses |
| Brand confusion (two identities and two locations) | Medium | Resolve before launch (§3) |
